Mixed reality for education — Learn by doing

In education, mixed reality can help students understand processes that are difficult to observe in a traditional classroom or laboratory. An electric circuit might display current flow, geometry can unfold above a desk and a simulation can give immediate feedback. The aim is not to replace teachers or hands-on learning, but to add new ways to explore and experiment.

From explanation to experience

An effective activity begins with a measurable learning goal: identify a variable, compare results, diagnose a problem or explain a mathematical relationship. A scenario is then created in which student decisions produce visible consequences. Hand tracking may support direct manipulation of virtual parts without controllers, subject to accessibility and usability testing.

Applications in science and mathematics

Possible demonstrations include assembling and testing a simple electric circuit, exploring different resistance values or understanding geometry with familiar spatial references. These are learning concepts and prototypes, not claims of programmes currently deployed at a named institution. Educational design requires collaboration with teachers and evaluation.

  • Reveal invisible phenomena and relationships between cause and effect.
  • Practice procedures with immediate feedback.
  • Build activities educators can integrate, review and assess.

Common questions

Does this replace physical laboratories?

No. Mixed reality complements practical laboratories and teaching, and can support learning where equipment is limited.

Can an activity be adapted to a specific subject?

Yes. Content and interactions should match the curriculum, learner age and educational environment.
